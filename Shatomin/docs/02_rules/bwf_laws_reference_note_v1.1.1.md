## Purpose

This note is a high-level reference map for Shatomin.

This is not a full copy of the official laws.
It is a Shatomin-friendly summary and routing guide for future RAG use.

## 1. Court and Lines

Relevant questions:

- Is the shuttle in or out?
- Does the line count as in?
- What if the shuttle lands partly on the line?

Shatomin answer principle:

- Lines are part of the area they define.
- If the shuttle touches the line, it is in.
- If the shuttle lands completely outside the boundary, it is out.
- Official matches follow line judge, umpire, and referee decisions.

## 2. Scoring

Relevant questions:

- Is the game to 15 or 21?
- What is rally point scoring?
- What happens at deuce?
- How many games are in a match?

Shatomin answer principle:

- The side winning a rally scores one point.
- Match and game format can depend on the tournament and rule version.
- Do not overstate one scoring system as universal.
- Always advise checking tournament regulations for official matches.

## 3. Change of Ends

Relevant questions:

- When do players change ends?
- What if players forgot to change ends?

Shatomin answer principle:

- Players change ends according to the match format and scoring system used.
- If an end-change mistake is discovered, official rules or the umpire determine how to correct it.
- For practice, correct it calmly when noticed.

## 4. Service

Relevant questions:

- Can the server touch the line?
- Can the receiver touch the line?
- Can the server move the feet?
- What if the receiver is not ready?
- Does the serve need to be diagonal?

Shatomin answer principle:

- Server and receiver stand in diagonally opposite service courts.
- They should not touch the boundary lines of those service courts.
- Some part of both feet should remain in contact with the court and stay still until the serve is delivered.
- The server should not serve before the receiver is ready.
- If the receiver tries to return the serve, the receiver is generally treated as ready.
- Official matches follow service judge, umpire, referee, and tournament regulations.

## 5. Singles

Relevant questions:

- Which side does the server use in singles?
- Who serves after winning a rally?

Shatomin answer principle:

- When the server's score is even, serve from the right service court.
- When the server's score is odd, serve from the left service court.
- If the server wins a rally, the server scores and serves again from the alternate court.
- If the receiver wins a rally, the receiver scores and becomes the new server.

## 6. Doubles

Relevant questions:

- Who serves next in doubles?
- Which side should the server stand on?
- Do both players change sides every rally?

Shatomin answer principle:

- The serving side's score decides right or left.
- Even score means right service court.
- Odd score means left service court.
- If the serving side wins, the same server serves again from the other service court.
- If the receiving side wins, it scores and becomes the new serving side.
- Players do not change service courts unless their side wins a point while serving.

## 7. Service Court Errors

Relevant questions:

- What if the wrong player served?
- What if someone served from the wrong side?

Shatomin answer principle:

- A service court error can involve serving or receiving out of turn or from the wrong court.
- When discovered, official rules or the umpire determine how to correct it.
- For practice, correct the order calmly when noticed.

## 8. Faults

Relevant questions:

- What is a fault?
- Is net touch a fault?
- Is double hit a fault?
- What if the shuttle hits the ceiling or wall?

Shatomin answer principle:

Common faults include:

- incorrect service
- shuttle lands out
- shuttle fails to pass the net
- shuttle touches ceiling or side wall
- shuttle touches a player or clothing
- player touches the net during play
- same player hits twice in succession
- unfair obstruction or distraction

Keep explanations short and avoid listing too many examples unless asked.

## 9. Lets

Relevant questions:

- What is a let?
- When is a rally replayed?
- What if the receiver was not ready?

Shatomin answer principle:

- A let means the rally is played again.
- It may occur when play is disturbed, the receiver was not ready, the shuttle breaks, or the umpire decides replay is needed.
- Official matches follow umpire and referee decisions.

## 10. Shuttle Not in Play

Relevant questions:

- When does a rally end?
- Is the shuttle still in play after touching the ground?

Shatomin answer principle:

- Once the shuttle is no longer in play, the rally is over.
- Common reasons include touching the court surface, a fault being called, or a let being called.
- Keep the answer simple for children.

## 11. Continuous Play and Misconduct

Relevant questions:

- Can players delay the game?
- What happens for bad behaviour?
- Can someone damage the shuttle?

Shatomin answer principle:

- Players should play continuously and behave appropriately.
- Delays, offensive behaviour, or misconduct may be penalized.
- Official matches follow umpire and referee decisions.

## 12. Officials and Appeals

Relevant questions:

- Who makes the final decision?
- Can a player challenge a call?
- What does the umpire do?

Shatomin answer principle:

- The referee is in overall charge of the tournament or event.
- The umpire is in charge of the match and court.
- A service judge may call service faults.
- A line judge calls in or out for assigned lines.
- Official decisions on points of fact are generally final, subject to specific appeal or review systems.

## Shatomin Routing Guide

When the user asks about:

- "line", "in", "out" -> use Court and Lines
- "serve", "receiver", "foot", "diagonal" -> use Service
- "doubles", "who serves", "right or left" -> use Doubles
- "score", "15", "21", "deuce" -> use Scoring
- "net", "double hit", "ceiling", "wall" -> use Faults
- "replay", "receiver not ready", "disturbance" -> use Lets
- "umpire", "line judge", "referee" -> use Officials and Appeals
- "delay", "bad behaviour", "shuttle damage" -> use Continuous Play and Misconduct

## Do Not Do

Shatomin should not:

- quote long official rule text
- pretend to replace the umpire
- decide a specific tournament rule without the tournament document
- argue against an official
- give a long legal explanation to children
- say that one scoring format is always used in every tournament
